Converge and Signal dot, refined

Six takes on each direction. Converge works on the feeling of two sessions syncing toward one point. Signal dot turns the dot of the i into a scope, kept light enough to sit inside a wordmark.

Direction F

Signal dot

The drawn wordmark stays the same; only the dot of the i changes. The small icon is the i alone, the same way the dot shows up as a scope while aim is watching.

Ring F1

A thin ring around the accent dot, lighter than the letter strokes so it reads as a sight sitting above the word.

Ticks F2

Four short crosshair ticks and no ring. The most minimal scope: the dot looks locked on without any extra outline.

Open ring F3

The ring broken at the four cardinal points, the classic scope reticle, drawn delicately. Feels precise and a little technical.

Halo F4

The dot with a soft glow instead of a hard ring, like a status light that just came on. The prettiest and the least literal.

Focus corners F5

Camera focus brackets around the dot: aim has found something and is showing it to you. Modern, and clearly different from a gun sight.

Bullseye F6

Solid accent rings instead of outlines. The heaviest dot, so it holds up best at 16 px and on busy backgrounds.

One thing worth trying next: the two directions can meet. B3's faded-and-full chevrons as the icon, F's wordmark beside it, with the same accent dot carried through both. Tell me which numbers you like and I'll combine or refine them.
