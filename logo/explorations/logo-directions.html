Six directions for the aim mark

Each one is a single ink colour plus one accent dot or shape, so it works as a terminal-sized favicon, a plugin icon and a wordmark. The accent always marks the same idea: the place where your work meets someone else's.

aim

Overlap A

Two sessions as two rings; the shared part lights up. It is the product in one shape: aim finds the intersection between your work and a teammate's.

Strongest meaning · reads well down to 16 px
aim

Converge B

Two terminal prompts pointing at the same spot. Two agents heading for the same problem, caught before they collide. Feels native next to Claude Code.

Most developer-flavoured · simple at every size
aim

Ping C

A dot sending a signal. aim speaks up on its own, quietly, while you work. Good for notifications and the moment the picker appears.

Best as an alert badge · close to generic "broadcast"
aim

Reticle D

The literal reading of the name: a crosshair with the accent on target. Instantly understood, but it says "aim" more than it says what aim does.

Most literal · many products already use a target
aim

Link E

You (open ring) and the teammate who already did it (filled), joined by a dotted thread. Puts the people at the centre: aim's job ends in a conversation.

Warmest · the dotted arc softens below 24 px
aim

Signal dot F

A drawn lowercase wordmark where the dot of the i is the accent. The icon is that i on its own, its dot held in a ring like a sight. Quietest, and the most ownable.

Wordmark-first · icon is the i, works as an app tile

My pick is Overlap (A) for meaning, or Signal dot (F) if you want aim to look calm and wordmark-led. Pick one and I'll refine its proportions and export SVG and PNG files for the plugin, the README and Loom.
